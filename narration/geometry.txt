A single image does not uniquely determine scene scale. In the pinhole camera model, projection depends on X over Z and Y over Z. Scaling every coordinate by the same positive factor leaves these ratios unchanged. Learned cues such as occlusion, perspective, and familiar objects help a model infer plausible geometry, but they do not make the answer unique. Relative depth describes near and far structure. Relative inverse depth is a proximity-like value; higher means closer in our common comparison convention. Metric depth has physical units. These quantities must stay separate. The viewer colors each map using its own range, so matching colors across maps do not imply matching distances. Mirrors and glass introduce another question: are we describing the physical surface or the scene visible through reflection or transmission?
