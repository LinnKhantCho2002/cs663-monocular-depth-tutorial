Monocular depth estimation
==========================
This project is about estimating depth from one photograph. I compared Depth Anything V2 Small, DepthAnything-AC Small, and Depth Anything 3 Small on the same images. One example is a car mirror: V2 and AC get an annotated near and far pair wrong, while DA3 gets it right. That difference can be easy to miss in the full maps. The tutorial starts with camera geometry and the meaning of relative depth, then explains how pretrained models are adapted for depth estimation. After that, you can explore the comparison and the sensor-reference examples. The browser loads predictions saved from local runs. It gives you controls for inspecting them, while the accompanying Python implementation handles fresh inference. Allow about twenty to twenty-seven minutes for the tutorial and examples.


Depth, scale, and the camera
============================
Think about a chair in front of a wall. A photograph often gives enough clues to order them, but their physical distances are less certain. The camera equation explains part of the problem: projection depends on X divided by Z and Y divided by Z. Doubling every scene coordinate leaves those ratios unchanged. Relative depth captures near and far structure on an arbitrary scale. In the inverse-depth convention used here, a larger value means closer. Metric depth reports physical distance with a defined camera convention. The color display normalizes each map separately, so equal colors across models do not imply equal distances. Glass and mirrors add a surface question: are we estimating the nearby material or the scene visible through it? Keep that question in mind when looking at the marked points.


How the models estimate depth
=============================
The model first extracts image features, then a decoder turns those features into a dense prediction. A transformer can combine information from patches across the image, and the decoder brings together overall layout and local detail. Depth Anything V2 trains students using depth pseudo-labels from a teacher. Marigold adapts a diffusion image model to generate depth. Metric3Dv2 and Depth Pro target physical distance. AC adapts a pretrained depth model to preserve geometry under appearance changes, while DA3 studies geometry from any number of views. DepthART explores smaller models and camera-specific adaptation. I ran the V2, AC, and DA3 Small checkpoints. The other methods are part of the literature review. This comparison uses pretrained weights; a controlled fine-tuning experiment would need separate training and test data.


Evaluating the predictions
==========================
The comparison uses fifteen DA-2K photographs with thirty-five annotated near and far pairs. Each model processes the original images, two dimming conditions, and a blurred condition. The same coordinates are scored each time. V2 and AC both get thirty-four clean pairs right. Under strong dimming, AC keeps thirty-four and V2 keeps thirty-two. Six NYU indoor scenes provide a second evaluation using sensor depth. Because the predictions are relative, the code fits a scale and shift before computing error. The fit uses the same reference pixels that are scored, so the result measures shape agreement after oracle alignment. It does not demonstrate raw meter accuracy. The evaluation page gives the mask, transformations, metrics, timing setup, and limits of the small sample. The download lets you recompute one saved kitchen example.


Exploring the depth maps
========================
Start with the car mirror, original input, and pair two. Switch between V2, AC, and DA3 and compare the model's answer with the source annotation. Reveal the map and adjust the overlay to inspect the region. You can move probes A and B with a click or the arrow keys. The profile follows the image line between them. Higher inverse depth means closer within the selected prediction. Free probes use a reduced display grid, while annotated-pair scores use exact original-resolution values. Next, compare a room under original and strongly dimmed input, or inspect a glass rim. In the NYU explorer, switch between the sensor reference, aligned prediction, error, and scoring mask. Results and next steps
======================
V2 and AC have the same clean pair count in this study. AC retains more correct pairs under strong digital dimming and blur. DA3 Small loses more pairs under those transformations, but gets a clean mirror pair right that the other two miss. Looking at individual examples therefore adds information to the totals. The six NYU scenes compare dense shape after reference-based alignment. Model choice also depends on the application: editing needs clean boundaries, reconstruction needs compatible geometry, and a small device needs measured memory and latency. My next experiments would use an independent camera-specific test set, physical exposure changes, and a controlled adaptation setup with a clean-domain check. Return to one example and explain the surface, output units, and reference. That connects the method to an actual prediction.
