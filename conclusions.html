What changed under difficult inputs?

V2 and AC each get 34 of 35 clean pairs right. Strong digital dimming leaves AC at 34 and V2 at 32; blur leaves them at 33 and 32. The adapted checkpoint preserves more of the annotated ordering on these selected images.

DA3-Small gets 33 clean pairs right, falling to 25 under strong dimming and 24 under blur. Its correct answer on the clean mirror pair shows a case that the overall count hides.

V2 and AC also have similar mean aligned errors on the six NYU scenes, while DA3's is higher. Those scores measure shape after fitting to the reference, as described in Evaluation.

Choosing for an application

A photographic blur effect needs a useful foreground mask. A missing glass rim or chair leg creates an editing artifact even if the room layout looks right.

Reconstruction and robotics need physical dimensions and often consistency across views or time. Camera calibration, output units, and sensor support become part of the model choice. Metric3Dv2, Depth Pro, and DA3 provide different approaches to those needs.

On a small device, memory and latency matter alongside accuracy. The warmed laptop medians were about 0.123 seconds for V2, 0.170 for AC, and 0.412 for DA3 on one indoor image. A phone test would need its own loading, memory, energy, and sustained-performance measurements. DepthART offers a recent method to investigate.

The next experiments

I would first collect a separate camera-specific test set with measured depth or independently annotated ordering. Splitting the data before tuning would keep the final test out of model selection.

I would then compare digital dimming with photographs of a fixed scene captured at different exposures, recording the camera settings. That would test how well the synthetic perturbations represent actual low-light capture.

For fine-tuning, I would keep an untouched baseline and test clean scenes as well as difficult inputs. Varying one loss term at a time could help identify what the consistency and spatial constraints contribute.
